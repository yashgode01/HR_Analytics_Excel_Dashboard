# HR Analytics Dashboard Using Excel

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing employee data using Microsoft Excel to identify workforce trends, understand employee attrition and generate meaningful HR insights.

The project uses data cleaning, Excel formulas, PivotTables, PivotCharts and interactive slicers to build an HR Analytics Dashboard that helps users analyze employee count, attrition, salary distribution and departmental workforce patterns.

## Objectives

- Analyze employee attrition and retention.
- Calculate the overall employee attrition rate.
- Compare attrition across departments and overtime groups.
- Analyze average monthly salary by employee attrition status.
- Identify job roles with the highest number of employees.
- Identify employees earning above their department's average salary.
- Calculate each department's share of total monthly payroll.
- Build an interactive HR dashboard using PivotTables and PivotCharts.

## Tools and Technologies

- **Microsoft Excel**
- Excel formulas: `COUNTIF`, `COUNTIFS`, `AVERAGEIF`, `SUMIF`, `FILTER`, `UNIQUE`, and `SORTBY`
- PivotTables and PivotCharts
- Slicers and interactive filters
- Excel 365 dynamic array functions
- Data cleaning and data analysis

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The dashboard focuses on the following key metrics:

- Total Employees
- Employees Who Left
- Employee Attrition Rate
- Average Monthly Salary

## Key Analysis

### 1. Employee Attrition Analysis
Calculated the number of employees who left the organization and the overall attrition rate.

### 2. Department-wise Attrition
Compared employee attrition across departments to identify areas that may require retention strategies.

### 3. Salary Analysis
Compared average monthly income between employees who left and employees who stayed.

### 4. Overtime Analysis
Compared attrition rates between employees who work overtime and those who do not.

### 5. Job Role Analysis
Identified the top five job roles with the highest employee counts.

### 6. Employee Salary Benchmarking
Identified employees whose monthly salary is higher than their department's average salary.

### 7. Department-wise Payroll Analysis
Calculated each department's total monthly payroll and its percentage share of the company's total monthly payroll.

## Dashboard Features

- KPI cards for key HR metrics
- Department-wise attrition analysis
- Overtime-based attrition comparison
- Employee distribution by job role
- Attrition analysis by age group and gender
- Interactive slicers for Department, Gender, Job Role and Overtime
- Dynamic reporting using PivotTables and PivotCharts

## Key Findings

The analysis is designed to help identify workforce patterns, including departments with higher attrition rates, differences in attrition between overtime groups, salary differences between employees who left and stayed and departments with the largest payroll contribution.

# HR Analytics: The Story Behind the Numbers

## 1. The Workforce at a Glance

Every organization depends on its people, but understanding workforce stability is essential for long-term success.

Our HR Analytics dashboard analyzes **1,480 employees** and brings together key indicators such as employee attrition, average salary, age-group distribution, and departmental workforce size.

The dashboard provides a starting point for understanding where the organization stands and where HR teams may need to investigate further.

## 2. Employee Attrition: Understanding the Challenge

The organization recorded **238 employee departures**, representing an overall attrition rate of **16.08%**.

In simple terms, approximately 16 out of every 100 employees left the organization during the period represented by the dataset.

This finding highlights an important workforce challenge. Employee departures can increase recruitment costs, create knowledge gaps, and place additional pressure on existing employees.

The next step for HR would be to investigate why employees are leaving by examining factors such as job role, overtime, salary, tenure, and department.

## 3. Departmental Workforce: Where Are the Employees?

The dashboard shows that the workforce is distributed unevenly across three departments:

- **Research & Development:** 967 employees
- **Sales:** 450 employees
- **Human Resources:** 63 employees

Research & Development represents the largest department, followed by Sales and Human Resources.

This distribution matters when interpreting attrition. A department with more employees may have more departures simply because its workforce is larger. Therefore, HR should compare attrition rates alongside departure counts rather than relying on counts alone.

## 4. Age-Group Analysis: Understanding Workforce Distribution

The dashboard compares employee attrition across five age groups: 18–25, 26–35, 36–45, 46–55, and 55+.

The visual shows the largest bar for the 26–35 age group, followed by the 36–45 group. However, the chart should be checked to confirm whether it represents total employees or employees who left.

Understanding age-group patterns can help HR investigate career development, compensation, work-life balance, and retention needs across different career stages.

Age alone should not be treated as the cause of attrition.

## 5. Salary Analysis: Comparing Departments and Job Levels

The dashboard compares salary levels across departments and job levels, helping identify differences in compensation distribution.

Research & Development has the largest workforce, so its overall salary expenditure may also be substantial. However, workforce size alone does not determine average salary or payroll costs.

HR can use this analysis to investigate compensation differences between job levels and departments, check internal salary consistency, and identify areas where additional benchmarking may be useful.
